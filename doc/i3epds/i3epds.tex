  \subsection{Numeric Representation}
    Our design uses fixed-point representation. The bit size and point location are arbitrary and chosen during synthesis to enable a trade-off between performance and precision.

    The choice between fixed-point and floating-point was not trivial. Fixed-point can represent values in the range $2^{{\pm}n}$, where $n$ is the number of bits; while floating-point representations disperse their values in a wider exponential range, increasing precision for values closer to zero. Recent FPGA designs have also included floating-point arithmetic cells, bringing performance closer to that of a fixed-point application.

  \subsection{Complex Multiplication}\label{ssec:cmul}
    Naively, Complex-domain multiplication implies a computation task 4x larger than Real-domain multiplication, due to the nature of Complex numbers (Equation~\ref{eq:cmul-rect}).

    \begin{equation}\label{eq:cmul-rect}
      (a+bi)(c+di) = (ac-bd) + (ad+bc)i
    \end{equation}

    Instead, we can represent the same operation in polar coordinates (Equation~\ref{eq:cmul-pol}) reducing the total operation count to 1 Real multiplication and 1 Real addition, which are independent and thus parallelizable.
    
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:cmul-pol}
      A\phase{\alpha_1} \cdot B\phase{\alpha_2} = AB\phase{\alpha_1+\alpha_2}
    \end{equation}
